# Competitive Analysis Methodology

## Positioning Analysis Framework

For each competitor, map their positioning:

| Dimension | Questions |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Target customer** | Who do they sell to? (segment, size, geography) |
| **Category** | How do they define their category? |
| **Key differentiation** | What do they claim is unique? |
| **Proof points** | What evidence do they use? (customers, metrics, awards) |
| **Value promise** | What outcome do they promise? |

## Win/Loss Analysis Patterns

When analyzing why customers chose a competitor:

| Factor | Questions to Investigate |
|--------|--------------------------|
| **Feature gaps** | What specific capabilities were missing? |
| **Integration** | Did existing tech stack influence? (SAP shop stays SAP) |
| **Price** | Was total cost of ownership a factor? |
| **Ecosystem** | Did partner/SI ecosystem matter? |
| **Trust** | Did incumbent relationship influence? |
| **Timeline** | Was time-to-value a deciding factor? |

## Strategic Response Options

For each competitive gap identified:

| Response | When to Use |
|----------|-------------|
| **Build** | Gap is strategic, customers demand it, feasible to build |
| **Partner** | Need exists but building isn't core competency |
| **Position** | Gap exists but our approach is genuinely better for our target customer |
| **Accept** | Gap is real but not worth addressing (wrong segment, low demand) |

## Competitive Intelligence Sources

### Product-Specific
- Gartner Magic Quadrant for your product category
- IDC MarketScape for your vertical
- G2/TrustRadius reviews (filter for your vertical)
- Competitor community forums and customer discussion boards
- Salesforce feedback portal for competitive feature requests

### Analysis Quality Checklist
- Are claims based on evidence (docs, reviews, customer feedback) or assumptions?
- Is the comparison fair? (comparing GA features to GA features, not roadmap)
- Are we acknowledging genuine competitor strengths, not just weaknesses?
- Is the audience-appropriate? (sales needs battlecards, leadership needs strategy)
